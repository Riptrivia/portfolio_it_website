Field guide / Responsible technology

E-waste,
handled well.

A quick-start process for deciding what to reuse, repair, donate, recycle, or securely retire—without losing track of data, batteries, or equipment.

01 / Triage

Start with condition.

Do not begin by assuming old means useless. Identify what the device is, whether it powers on, what is missing, and whether repair is proportionate to its likely next use.

CHECK

Physical safety

Look for swollen batteries, heat damage, sharp edges, liquid exposure, corrosion, or a damaged power supply before applying power.

IDENTIFY

Device and ownership

Record the model, serial or asset tag when appropriate, included accessories, ownership, and whether the device contains storage.

TEST

Basic function

Confirm power, display, charging, keyboard or touch input, ports, network access, storage health, and obvious noise or thermal issues.

DECIDE

Next useful role

Consider repair, parts harvesting, donation, a lightweight Linux install, offline lab use, or certified recycling.

02 / Data

Protect information first.

Storage devices can hold account details, documents, browser sessions, photos, and organization data long after the computer stops being useful.

  • Back up only the files the owner is authorized to keep.
  • Sign out of accounts and remove the device from account-management portals where applicable.
  • Use an appropriate secure-erase process for the storage type and reuse plan.
  • If erasure cannot be verified, remove the drive and keep it under control for approved destruction or separate handling.
  • Do not donate, sell, or recycle a data-bearing device until the data decision is documented.
Encryption is helpful, but disposition still matters.

Encrypted storage can reduce exposure; it does not replace account removal, approved wiping, verification, or physical control of a drive that cannot be sanitized.

03 / Batteries

Isolate battery risk.

Lithium batteries need special handling. Stop using a battery that is swollen, leaking, punctured, unusually hot, or giving off odor. Keep it away from heat and combustible materials, avoid crushing or bending it, and follow local hazardous-waste or battery-recycling instructions.

Do not place lithium batteries in household trash or ordinary recycling.

Local rules and collection options vary. Use an approved battery drop-off or recycler, and ask the receiving location how terminals and damaged packs must be prepared.

04 / Decision

Choose the highest useful path.

REUSE

Keep it working

Clean it, replace low-cost wear items, upgrade memory or storage, install supported software, and validate the device for a specific use.

DONATE

Transfer a complete device

Confirm the recipient accepts the model, remove accounts and data, include a safe charger, and disclose limitations honestly.

PARTS

Recover serviceable components

Label known-good memory, drives, adapters, screens, cables, and fasteners. Keep unknown or unsafe components out of reuse stock.

RECYCLE

Use an accountable channel

Select a recycler appropriate to the equipment and your local requirements. Confirm acceptance, data-handling expectations, and battery rules before drop-off.

05 / Handoff

Prepare a clean transfer.

  • Remove stickers, labels, or markings that expose personal or organizational information.
  • Clean the device without spraying liquid directly into openings.
  • Bundle only safe, compatible chargers and accessories.
  • Describe known defects, missing parts, passwords, and licensing limits.
  • Obtain a receipt or handoff record when accountability matters.

06 / Record

Close the loop.

A lightweight record prevents devices from becoming mystery inventory. Capture the item, identifier if appropriate, date, data action, disposition, destination, and the person or process that confirmed completion.

Minimum record

Device · identifier · condition · data status · battery status · final path · date.

Final verification

Confirm the equipment actually left storage, reached its destination, and no removed data-bearing parts remain untracked.
